How To Play

Goal: Clear the field by placing a flag over each of the mines

Left Click

Uncovers Square

Right Click

Places Flag

Start a new game by pressing any random square to reveal what is underneath. It is not gaurenteed that the first click will not be a bomb so your first press may result in a loss.

When clearing non-mine squares a number may appear [1, 2, 3, . . . 8]. That number represents how many mines are in adjacent squares, if a revealed square has a 3, that indicates there are 3 mines in the surrounding squares.

If you are certain a square has a mine underneath, right click places a flag and makes it unclickable. Right click again to remove a flag if needed.

When a number has an equal number of flags surrounding it, you may middle click to reveal all remaining squares adjacent to that number. This can help speed up game play but be warned, if the flags are not in the right place, it will result in a loss.

To start a new game, simply select the difficulty you would like to play at the top of the screen. The difficulties increase by 10 mines each time starting with easy at 10 and hard at 30.

Once all non mine squares have been clicked you win!

Interesting Facts

  • Minesweeper was created in 1990 by Microsoft and introduced in Windows 3.11, with speculation that the game design was largely inspired by Mined-Out, a game released in 1983. The two games share grid style and a simlar display for number of mines in adjacent squares. While never fully confirmed by Microsoft, Minesweeper's fame grew exponentially in comparison due to it coming standard on all Windows machines at the time depsite having less features.
  • Over the years there have been several varieties of Minesweeper created. One of them being available in the non-Japanese versions of Pokemon HeartGold and SoulSilver known as "Voltorb Flip". It features a 5 x 5 grid where players flip cards to reveal hidden numbers where the Pokemon Voltorb acts as the mines.
